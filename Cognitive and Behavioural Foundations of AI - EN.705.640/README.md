# Cognitive and Behavioral Foundations of AI

This folder contains my research project for the **Cognitive and Behavioral Foundations for Artificial Intelligence (EN.705.640)** course, part of the Johns Hopkins Engineering for Professionals (EP) program.

---

## Course Overview

This course explores the cognitive and behavioral principles underlying artificial intelligence, examining how insights from psychology, neuroscience, and cognitive science inform the design, evaluation, and ethical development of AI systems. Through readings, discussions, and a major research project, the course challenges students to critically analyze how AI systems mimic, diverge from, or extend human cognitive and behavioral capabilities.

---

## Major Research Project

### Title
**Evaluating Artificial and Alternative Intelligence: A New Framework for AI Evaluation**

### Summary
This research proposal investigates how the definition of AI shapes its evaluation. Traditional metrics like the Turing Test focus narrowly on human mimicry, overlooking the superhuman and hybrid capabilities of modern AI systems.  

The paper proposes the **Multidimensional Intelligence Evaluation Framework (MIEF)** — a holistic framework that integrates:
- Human-centric replication
- Superhuman capabilities
- Adaptability
- Sustainability
- Ethical alignment
- Value added  

By moving beyond anthropocentric benchmarks, the MIEF enables a balanced assessment that celebrates both human-like and nonhuman strengths, guiding AI development toward ethically responsible and societally beneficial applications.

---

## Core Contributions

- **Literature Synthesis**: Integrates perspectives from Negrotti’s *Alternative Intelligence*, Pease & Colton’s *Computational Creativity*, AlphaFold, sepsis prediction models, and animal-inspired optimization algorithms.
- **Framework Design**: Introduces the MIEF framework as a comprehensive tool for evaluating both human-mimicking and beyond-human AI systems.
- **Critical Analysis**: Explores the limitations of human intelligence as a benchmark, highlighting cognitive biases and evolutionary constraints.
- **Practical Implications**: Discusses how MIEF can guide responsible AI development, balancing innovation, sustainability, and ethics.
